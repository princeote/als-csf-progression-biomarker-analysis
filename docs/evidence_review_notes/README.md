# Evidence Review Notes

## Purpose

This directory contains evidence-review documents developed during construction of the literature-guided protein panels evaluated in this project.

These documents summarize published evidence used to evaluate candidate biomarkers, biological mechanisms, assay availability, and panel composition before final model evaluation.

They are intended to document the scientific reasoning behind panel development rather than serve as a formal systematic review.

---

## Contents

### 01 – Literature-Guided Candidate Screen

Reviews human CSF biomarkers reported in the ALS progression literature and identifies candidate proteins based on:

- progression-rate evidence
- survival associations
- biological relevance
- CSF detectability
- assay compatibility

---

### 02 – Mechanism-Guided Candidate Expansion

Evaluates additional proteins that broaden biological coverage across major ALS pathways, including:

- neuroinflammation
- complement/coagulation
- proteostasis
- extracellular matrix biology
- axonal injury

---

### 03 – Assay-Available Panel Modification

Documents how the literature-guided panel was adapted after comparing candidate proteins with the proteins measurable in the available mass-spectrometry dataset.

Unavailable proteins were replaced only when assay-available alternatives preserved the intended biological mechanisms.

---

### 04 – Tenascin-R and Contactin-1 ECM Rationale

Summarizes the evidence supporting inclusion of Tenascin-R and Contactin-1 as extracellular matrix and neuronal adhesion markers.

These proteins were included because of their mechanistic relevance and human CSF detectability rather than because they are established ALS progression biomarkers.

---

### 05 – Perineuronal Net Biomarker Review

Reviews proteins involved in extracellular matrix remodeling and perineuronal-net biology, with emphasis on Neurocan, Tenascin-R, Brevican, and related structural biomarkers.

---

### 06 – Directional Evidence Review

Summarizes published evidence regarding whether proteins tend to be increased or decreased in faster ALS progression.

This document was used to understand biological directionality and interpretation of panel members rather than to select proteins based on model performance.

---

## Notes

These documents summarize published literature that informed development of the literature-guided biomarker panels.

Final model evaluation, feature comparison, and performance assessment were performed separately using leakage-controlled repeated nested cross-validation.

The primary manuscript analyses should be interpreted using the manuscript methods and cited primary literature rather than these working evidence summaries alone.
